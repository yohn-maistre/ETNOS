# What Strata is, what it gives a people, and its building blocks

19 November 2026. A step back, asked for by the owner: what the platform is as a whole, what it provides for a people, and which building blocks (primitives) it has. It also settles how **peoples, languages and private spaces** relate. Marks: *(proposal)* for suggestions the owner can overturn, **live / built / planned** for status, as in `proposal/claims-2026-11-18.md`. Names follow `naming.md`.

## 1. In one paragraph

See `what-strata-is.md` for the whole picture. In short: Strata is a social network for a region and its peoples (ETNOS for Tanah Papua). Conversation comes first; a sourced wiki, a community dictionary and an open observatory grow underneath it; assistants (Aksara) do the library work under each community's rules. This document covers the building blocks and how peoples, languages and private spaces relate.

## 4. Peoples, languages and private spaces

### 4.1 The owner's proposal

Consolidate language under the people (suku) or lineage:
- The private spaces (Strata private communities, and later private AT Protocol spaces) are the peoples' spaces.
- A language works like a subreddit's wiki inside its people's space, rather than as a free-standing topic, since a language usually belongs to a lineage of related groups rather than to three unrelated peoples at once.
- People can give reasons to join, as with private Lemmy instances.

### 4.2 What is right about it

- **For most of Tanah Papua's highland languages, people and language go together.** Mee people speak Mee (Ekari), and the language takes the people's name. A dictionary needs a home and someone responsible for it, and the people is the natural one.
- **It fits KKI 2023, idea 9:** communities are sovereign over the sources of their cultural knowledge. The people governs its language data.
- **It fits the technology.** A private space per people maps onto private AT Protocol spaces when they arrive, and onto a people's own data host later.
- **The join mechanism already exists.** Join requests carry a message, so "give a reason to join" needs no new code.

### 4.3 Where it needs care

1. **Not every language maps to one people.**
   - Papuan Malay is spoken by everyone and belongs to no one.
   - Several Dani languages are spoken across groups with different names.
   - One people can have several dialects (Steltenpool's dictionary marks Tigi, Paniai, Kamu and Mapija forms of Mee).
   - Coastal languages travel with migration.
   - A strict tree breaks on these cases.
2. **The platform must not decide who belongs to a people.** If a language's dictionary is reachable only through membership in a people's space, the platform becomes a gatekeeper of identity. That is the registry the design rules forbid ("no registry of persons"; "AT identities do not grant community membership").

   Many people are of mixed descent, grew up in a city, or are relearning the language. Learners and researchers also need a way in.
3. **Who runs "the Mee space"?** Clans, dialect areas, churches and customary councils may each claim to speak for a people. The showcase plan (§7) chose that no room is "the official one", to prevent capture by whoever arrives first.
4. **A people is not a lineage.** Clans and marga are smaller, more sensitive groupings. Kinship stays out of public labels by rule.

### 4.4 Recommended structure *(proposal)*

Keep the owner's core idea, that **a language's living record belongs to its people**, but **link rather than nest**:

1. **People page (public).** A people becomes its own page kind, alongside place and language. The catalogue already notes that peoples are not yet pages of their own. The page carries:
   - the people's self-description first;
   - its places and languages, as claims with sources;
   - its public works and the dictionary entries its spaces have published.
2. **People space (private by default).** A group of a new kind, *people*, linked to the people page. It holds the people's private communities: general talk, dialect rooms (for example Mee · Tigi), a language room and, later, clan spaces. **The language's community dictionary lives here**, like a subreddit's wiki.
3. **Language page (public), linked to one or more people spaces.** It shows the published record (TransNewGuinea.org, Steltenpool) and the entries the linked spaces chose to publish, each with its source. Papuan Malay has no owning space: its dictionary is an open public community.
4. **More than one space per people is allowed.**
   - A people page lists every space that says it belongs to that people, each with its own description and member count.
   - A space can be marked as recognised by a customary institution (a lembaga adat, or the MRP's cultural role) only through a sourced claim, never by the platform.
5. **Joining.**
   - People request with a reason, as now.
   - Stewards approve.
   - A member can vouch ("dikenal oleh"; showcase plan §11).
   - The platform never infers membership from a name, a surname or a place, and never shows a person's people on their profile.
   - Membership in a people space is a membership, not an identity record, and the member list is visible to members only.
6. **A way in for learners.** Each language page has a public learners' community (*Belajar bahasa Mee*, already an example in the catalogue). The people space chooses what it publishes to learners.
7. **Clan spaces later.** These are hidden, invite-only communities inside a people space, moving to a clan's own data host in the roadmap. They never appear in public, and are never inferred.

### 4.5 Three kinds of private, and what each guarantees

| Kind | Who can read | Available |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Strata private community** | Members, and technically the operator of the deployment (ETNOS). The interface must say so (showcase plan §25.1) | Now |
| **Private AT Protocol space** | Members, through access control on a data host; the host's operator can still read it. Access control, not encryption | Public beta from 1 December |
| **A people's own data host** | Members and the people's chosen operator (for example a machine run by its own organisation) | Roadmap |

End-to-end encrypted group spaces are not planned yet. The design problems (group keys, recovery) are listed in `architecture.md`.

### 4.6 What it changes in the code *(proposal, for the coding agent)*

- Add `people` as a page (context) kind, seeded from the catalogue's peoples, with self-description fields and claims to places and languages.
- Add `people` as a group kind. Link a group to its people page with the existing label table (groups can already be labelled).
- Scope the community dictionary to a group (people space) or to a single public community (for languages without an owning people).
- On language pages: list linked spaces; show published entries only.
- On people pages: list spaces, with recognition shown only as a sourced claim.
- Tests: membership never visible to non-members; no people shown on profiles; published entries only from approved copies; withdrawal removes published copies.

## 5. Open questions for the owner

1. Is "people" (suku) the right top-level word, or should it be "masyarakat adat" where a customary body exists? Self-description may differ by people.
2. Should a people space be allowed to be hidden entirely, or always listed on its people page with a member count?
3. Who may create a people space: anyone with a second member (as now), or only after a request reviewed by ETNOS stewards?
4. Should published dictionary entries from a people space be credited to the space, or to the speakers who choose to be named?
