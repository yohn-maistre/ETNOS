# A relational knowledge landscape for the commons

Research and discussion note · 12 November 2026, Asia/Bangkok · **Planning only; no graph feature, ontology, storage migration or deployment is authorized by this note.**

This responds to the owner's request for an Indigenous communal human-and-bioscape knowledge landscape that grows from actual relationships, rather than dividing life into separate biology, culture, history and music shelves. The owner has earlier writings and further thoughts to discuss after the current implementation run. This document preserves that direction and supplies research for that discussion; it does not replace the owner's foundation or speak for Papuan communities.

Read alongside the [original foundation](../foundation/living-commons.md), [foundation guide](../foundation/README.md), [commons refinement](../foundation/commons-experience-2026-11-12.md), [architecture](../accounts-and-hosting/architecture.md), [execution plan](../foundation/execution-plan.md), and [current Wiki/clustering checkpoint](../knowledge/connected-wiki-clustering-2026-11-12.md). The foundation already treats categories as lenses over linked material that can grow through use, and an environmental observation as an artifact with a source, place, time, contributor and access conditions. This proposal makes those commitments more explicit; it does not originate them.

## Recommendation

Build toward an **evidence-bearing relational layer over the existing commons**, with reading pages and community-defined paths as the primary experience. An optional bounded graph can help people inspect relationships. A global force-directed graph should not become the main Wiki home or a compulsory representation of community knowledge.

Start from a situated question or contribution: a place name in someone's language, a recording, an observation, a fishing practice, a musical development, a displacement history, a market offering or a published article. Retain its source and permission conditions. Let people describe meaningful connections in their own terms. Add broad scientific or external identifiers where useful, without making them the definition of local meaning.

Three distinctions matter:

1. **Relationality is not the same thing as graph technology.** A database can connect thousands of things while erasing the responsibilities, perspective and authority that make those connections meaningful [1–4].
2. **A published relationship is a sourced assertion, not an unqualified universal truth.** Its author, vocabulary, place, time, evidence, interpretation and review state should remain inspectable [2,4,11].
3. **The right to publish a node does not imply the right to publish every relationship around it.** Governance applies to narratives, metadata, locations, relationships and derived outputs, not only attached files [3,5–9].

This is a product recommendation informed by the sources below, not a published or universally endorsed Indigenous ontology. Communities can choose not to digitize certain relationships at all.

## What the literature actually supports

This is a targeted narrative review, not a systematic review or an exhaustive account of Indigenous knowledge organization. Recent work was sought over **12 November 2021–12 November 2026**, retaining older foundational research. Exa discovery was followed by publisher, author-repository and official documentation checks. Search dates and article submission dates were not treated as publication dates. Some publisher pages supplied abstracts rather than full texts; the table marks the inference limits.

### Indigenous knowledge organization and situated design

| Source and evidence type | What it contributes | Limits and practical consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Littletree, Belarde-Lewis & Duarte (2020), “Centering Relationality”** [1]. Peer-reviewed conceptual knowledge-organization paper; publisher abstract and bibliographic record checked. | Identifies limits of conventional Euro-American knowledge organization and offers an epistemological model to help practitioners work across knowledge systems. | A conceptual/pedagogical contribution, not a ready-made schema or evaluation of a knowledge graph. Do not claim that importing a particular set of nodes implements its model. |
| **Belarde-Lewis et al. (2024), “Centering Relationality and CARE for Stewardship of Indigenous Research Data”** [2]. Peer-reviewed research paper; full-text sections reviewed. | DSISS case work and formal engagement with Indigenous scholars, community members and information professionals foreground ownership, trust and relational accountability in data stewardship. | Institutional, researcher and community interests can differ. A researcher account, community membership and authority over an item must remain different things. This study does not determine who can represent a Papuan language community. |
| **Reid et al. (2025), Kaitiaki Intelligence Platform** [3]. Peer-reviewed participatory design/research paper; full-text sections reviewed. | Work with **four Māori partners: two agribusiness collectives and two iwi**, combines biophysical sensing with place-based mātauranga, partner-defined indicators, relational communication and layered data security. It is an unusually close contemporary analogue for an environmental knowledge observatory. | The paper establishes conceptual/design foundations intended to support a later build; it does not demonstrate a finished AI platform's efficacy. Māori ontologies, kinship terms and metaphors are situated findings, not Papuan defaults. Transfer the practice of co-design and partner authority, not the vocabulary. |
| **Tengö et al. (2014), Multiple Evidence Base** [4]. Peer-reviewed *Ambio* Perspective; full-text sections reviewed. | Knowledge systems contribute evidence on their own terms. Assessment within knowledge systems can precede bringing complementary evidence together; agreements, contradictions and unanswered questions need not disappear. | This is a framework, not permission to present every assertion as equally substantiated. Distinguish measurement, observation, testimony, interpretation and inference without making one external system the automatic validator of all others. |
| **Carroll et al. (2020), CARE Principles** [5]. Peer-reviewed journal **practice paper** presenting a governance framework; publisher text checked. | Collective Benefit, Authority to Control, Responsibility and Ethics put people and purpose alongside data interoperability. Indigenous data includes environmental, territorial and nonhuman relations, not only traditional cultural objects. | CARE is not a software certification and does not decide local authority or consent procedures. Implement the actual community protocols rather than displaying a CARE badge. |
| **Jennings et al. (2025), “Governance of Indigenous data in open earth systems science”** [6]. Peer-reviewed *Nature Communications* **Perspective**; full-text sections reviewed. | Makes governance responsibilities visible for environmental/earth-system data and offers practical recommendations for open science. | A perspective/recommendations paper, not an access-control benchmark. A satellite layer or environmental dataset being downloadable does not settle all rights, interests or appropriate uses. |
| **Christen (2012; 2015)** [7,8]. Peer-reviewed archives/communication scholarship grounded in digital heritage practice; publisher records and source excerpts reviewed. | Explains why unconstrained openness is insufficient for Indigenous archives, and why community-specific, changing circulation protocols matter. | These accounts do not imply all Indigenous knowledge is secret, static, ceremonial or collectively uniform. Preserve ordinary public life as well as protected material; do not turn stereotypes into permission templates. |

The 2021 *Scientific Data* article on operationalizing CARE with FAIR [10] is useful **journal commentary**, rather than empirical validation of a deployed system. FAIR interoperability and purposeful, authorized access can coexist; “accessible” must not be equated with “all material public to everyone.” Neither FAIR nor CARE supplies a universal community governance process.

### A particularly relevant Melanesian example

**Schreyer, Longenecker & Wagner (2025)** document collaborative work with Kala-speaking communities in Morobe Province, **Papua New Guinea**, through river walks, reef-video interviews and adapted word-collection workshops [9]. The activities connect language with environments, subsistence, place histories and community knowledge experts.

The paper contains a direct warning against designing the shelves first: an initial environmental encyclopedia divided entries taxonomically into fish, plants and shells. The Kala research team rejected that organization in favor of **ocean and river**, with **village browsing** also requested. This does not establish ocean/river as the right structure for Strata. It demonstrates that apparently reasonable scientific categories can fail a community's intended way of finding and relating knowledge, and that the structure should be revisable by the people using it.

**Kik et al. (2021)** studied language and ethnobiological knowledge among 6,190 students representing 392 languages in Papua New Guinea [12]. The observational findings connect language use and ethnobiological skills, and describe local names that need not have established translations or scientific identifications. This supports preserving local terms and relationships without requiring a scientific or Wikidata match before they become usable. It does **not** show that a Wiki or AI assistant prevents language loss, nor establish prevalence or causality for Papua Indonesia.

These are regional methodological examples, not evidence about Meepago/Tigi governance or categories. This review has **not established a co-designed ontology for Meepago, a Papuan-wide consensus, or a validated Papuan observatory interface**. Community-specific Papuan work, the owner's earlier writing, local language practitioners and actual participating groups must inform that next discussion. Do not fill this gap with a synthesized “Indigenous worldview.”

## What existing systems teach us

### Local Contexts: make responsibilities visible, without pretending a label enforces them

Local Contexts' **Traditional Knowledge (TK)** Labels communicate community-specific protocols and responsibilities; **Biocultural (BC)** Labels communicate expectations around biocultural collections and data [13]. Labels can be customized and translated by the communities that govern them. Researchers and institutions use **Notices** to identify rights/interests and begin engagement; they should not invent a community's label or claim authority to approve it.

The official FAQ describes Labels and Notices as **extra-legal mechanisms that complement licenses**. They are not equivalent to copyright ownership, software authorization or a substitute for access controls. The Hub distributes Labels/Notices; it is not the archive itself. Customized community text and the Label icons also have their own rights and usage conditions [13].

For Strata, a future integration could preserve a Local Contexts project/Label identifier and show the approved community text with an item or approved derivative. It should respect display/use conditions and updated community decisions. **Do not copy or redesign their icons, auto-apply Labels, or use “TK” as a new permission enum.** A visible label can communicate expectations while actual API, storage, search, export and model access remain separately enforced.

### Mukurtu: one shared item can carry several situated records

Mukurtu's documented **Community Records** let different communities retain their own metadata and narratives around an item without editing away each other's contributions [14]. Records can use different cultural protocols; its documentation also assigns protocols to media assets and language recordings. This is stronger than attaching one supposedly definitive description to a file.

Useful lesson: a shared public photograph or named place may have several attributed interpretations and access conditions. Strata can learn from that separation while keeping its own contexts/community/Node model. Mukurtu's terminology and detailed membership rules should not be transplanted verbatim, and these documentation pages describe system behavior rather than establish a user-study result for Strata.

### Wikipedia, Wikidata and Wikibase: take sourced statements, not compulsory external authority

Wikipedia's reading experience and Wikidata's data model solve related but different problems. A sourced Wiki article is a readable synthesis; an item/property graph supports precise relationships. Neither replaces the other.

Wikidata/Wikibase statements can carry **qualifiers, references and ranks**, retaining more context than a bare subject–predicate–object edge [11,15]. That is a useful pattern for time-bounded or attributed assertions. References are supported but not magically supplied by the model; missing references remain missing. Preferred/normal/deprecated ranks are not probabilities, Indigenous authority decisions or Strata's editorial acceptance state.

Local identifiers should remain first-class. A possible external match is not identity. Avoid automatic `owl:sameAs` assertions or collapsing two communities' concepts just because names, embeddings or translations resemble one another. Some local concepts will have no external equivalent. Keep the original local term, its language, definition/context and attribution; a broad or related mapping can be explicitly weaker than equivalence.

**Public Wikidata structured data is CC0** [16]. Exporting to it is a publication and reuse decision, not a harmless internal synchronization step. Self-hosted Wikibase is different from publishing to Wikidata, but adopting its statement model still does not itself implement Strata's required access boundaries. The default plan does not require operating another service.

## A minimal model to discuss, not a new ontology to ship

The smallest useful addition is **a relationship assertion with evidence and context**, attached to existing objects. Keep the prose, recording or artifact that carries the knowledge; do not require users to translate it into triples before sharing it.

| Existing or candidate record | Purpose | Constraint |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Existing artifact, post, Wiki page/note, context, organization** | Stable things people can read or discuss. A current event, song, offer or observation can connect through these records. | Reuse canonical records. Commerce remains an ordinary post extension, not a separate universal knowledge category. |
| **Local concept/term, if needed** | A community-attributed meaning with multilingual names, variants, definition, recording and optional external mappings. | A language, ethnic identity, territory and account membership are not interchangeable. Multiple concepts can share a spelling; one concept can have several names. |
| **Relationship assertion** | A versioned claim that one thing relates to another in a stated way, or has a stated value. | Original relation wording, attributed source, method/perspective, relevant place/time, evidence links and review state survive projection. A newly inferred edge does not become accepted knowledge. |
| **Observation or event, when relevant** | Locates the act of observing, recording, performing, discussing or changing something. | Separate what was observed from later identification, interpretation and causal explanation. “No observation recorded” is not evidence of absence. |
| **Vocabulary/mapping record, only when useful** | Records a community's terms and deliberate links to other vocabularies. | Mapping has an author, purpose and review history. Never silently merge local schemes into one universal taxonomy. |

This is an implementation hypothesis. Not all rows require new tables. An initial relationship record could reference existing IDs, evidence and review receipts in SQLite; a graph is a projection, not a reason to replace the current database.

An assertion should be capable of retaining:

- **Meaning:** subject, local relation label/definition, object or literal; original language and wording; a narrative/source pointer when a triple would lose meaning.
- **Evidence:** source/version, passage or timecode where available, attributed person/group when permitted, method, and whether the relation is a reported claim, an observation or a steward inference.
- **Situatedness:** vocabulary/perspective, place or approved extent, observed/valid time distinct from recording/publishing time, season/era only when supported.
- **Accountability:** responsible editor/steward, review state, correction/history, permission/purpose conditions, current availability and any approved public derivative identity.

Do not force every contributor to fill this list. Existing upload/post/edit mechanisms can provide routine provenance; stewards can suggest additional structure, and people can correct it. Do not invent precision, ancestry, seasonal calendars or cultural authority from missing fields.

Illustrative **synthetic** path, not a factual assertion about Tigi:

`[published place article] → mentions → [locally named practice] → described in → [approved recording excerpt]`

Each arrow has its own source and access decision. The public path can be absent even if its endpoints are public. A steward's suggested connection appears as a suggestion until the appropriate editorial process accepts it.

### Select standards for the work they actually do

These are interoperability/reference tools, **not empirical evidence that one worldview is correct**. Map to a small selected subset only when an actual exchange or query needs it.

| Model or standard | Useful fit | What it should not become |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Wikibase statement pattern** [11,15] | Qualifiers, references, multiple statements, external identifiers; possible future public exchange. | A required new deployment, universal cultural schema, access-control system or confidence oracle. |
| **SKOS** [17], W3C Recommendation | Language-tagged labels, concept schemes, notes and deliberately differentiated cross-scheme mappings. | A requirement that every community use a hierarchy or that every similar term be equivalent. `exactMatch` also has inference consequences; use it only deliberately. |
| **PROV-O** [18], W3C Recommendation | Connect entities, activities and agents: recording → transcription → approved excerpt → Wiki synthesis; keep derivation inspectable. | Proof that a claim is true, an agent is authorized, a person consents, or a derivative may be public. Provenance metadata itself can be protected. |
| **CIDOC CRM**, ISO 21127 / community versions [19] | Rich event-centered documentation of cultural heritage, actors, objects, places and time. Helpful when a collection needs formal exchange. | A compulsory museum catalog for everyday posts or a mandate to represent all community life through institutional collection concepts. Pin an explicit stable version if adopted; the newest listed drafts are not automatically the ISO-corresponding release. |
| **CRMinf**, CRM SIG-approved extension [20] | More explicit premises, conclusions and reasoning activities when an actual research argument needs them. | Necessary scaffolding for every article or an automated machine that decides whose evidence counts. Its scope and approval are not the same as claiming it is itself ISO 21127. |
| **Darwin Core** [21], TDWG vocabulary | Biodiversity occurrences/events, identifications, taxon references and selected observation metadata for approved scientific exchange. | Definition of a local name or a reduction of relationships to species/use lists. A scientific identification and a community term can remain different records. Generalization/withholding terms do not enforce secrecy and can reveal that protected information exists. |
| **GeoSPARQL 1.1** [22], OGC standard | Represent/query geometries and spatial relations if RDF/spatial exchange is actually required. | Requirement to publish coordinates, single administrative boundary, ownership claim, or substitute for a culturally meaningful place. Existing approved GeoJSON/extents can suffice initially. |

RDF named graphs can organize datasets and attribution; **a named graph is not an access-control boundary by itself**. Graph databases, RDF serializations, SQLite tables and visualizations are separate choices. None needs to be selected to finish the current demo.

## Fit with Strata and independent Aksara Nodes

Keep the existing distinctions visible in implementation and ordinary UI:

| Primitive | Role in a knowledge landscape |
| --- | --- |
| **Context** | Describes a place, language, topic or other relationship lens. Many-to-many links allow administrative, customary and language geographies to overlap. It grants no membership, representation or source rights. |
| **Community** | People participate under rules and a defined audience; a language community can choose what to share, discuss and review. It can relate to several contexts without claiming to speak for all people connected to them. |
| **Organization** | A group with accountable representatives and explicit mandates, not an automatic authority over every associated community or cultural source. |
| **Audience / Space** | Controls participation/access where implemented; it is not a synonym for a language, ethnicity, context or graph namespace. Standard AT PDS publication is public; Spaces alpha access control is not encryption. |
| **Independent Aksara Node** | Custodies originals, internal workspace, language data and permitted agent operations under its own grants. Its local knowledge landscape need not be mirrored into Strata. |
| **Public Wiki** | Readable accepted and explicitly provisional pages in one home, with sources and review state. No separate “working knowledge” destination is needed. |
| **Steward** | Suggests links, prepares sourced drafts and may announce an entry under current editorial/posting mandates. It does not infer cultural authority or turn private language data into public context. |

The current public-only **Dalam pengembangan** projection, citations, section navigation, history and correction/promotion flows are a good starting point. A later relationship lens can attach to those pages without delaying them. Private local notes and pending proposals remain outside it.

The current optional Faiss **e-means** receipt/candidate pipeline can suggest **related public sources**, as a discovery aid. Similarity is not evidence of a relationship, equivalence of concepts, territorial membership, authority or an Indigenous classification. Do not name clusters as cultures, merge communities from them, or use embedding distance to certify a source. The current lexical baseline is not a validated multilingual semantic model. Public ranking and popularity likewise cannot decide factual validity, cultural importance or permission.

### Privacy is a property of the whole projection

These are requirements inherited from Strata's existing boundaries, with graph-specific consequences:

1. **Filter before retrieval, clustering, graph traversal and layout.** Public endpoints must not read protected originals or compute on a mixed private/public graph and hide labels afterward. Node degrees, counts, paths, “related” suggestions and empty silhouettes can disclose protected relations.
2. **Authorize edges independently.** Two public names can have a protected association. The existence of a private record, withheld term or sensitive site must not be exposed through a public stub, relation count or “request access” hint unless that disclosure itself is approved.
3. **Keep original and derivative identities distinct.** An authorized transcript excerpt or coarse public map is a new approved derivative; it does not open the original recording, coordinates or surrounding knowledge. Do not make private Node links resolvable through public graph/search URLs.
4. **Check current rights and purpose at effect time.** Permissions to view, quote, index, embed, train, export and publish are different decisions. Node custody and community membership do not automatically authorize all of them.
5. **Make updates and withdrawal real.** Stale relationships, embeddings, search indexes, cached graph layouts and aggregates must become unavailable or be regenerated when current source rights/versions change. Retraction/correction receipts should not themselves reveal a protected source. Already copied public data cannot be promised retroactive erasure from other people's systems.
6. **Avoid universal authority accounts.** There may be several legitimate contributors and contested perspectives, with different responsibilities. No automatic ontology merge, agent vote or popularity score settles collective consent.

Not every legitimate community request can be solved by a software flag; the UI must not promise an operational control that the Node, storage and publication pathway do not yet enforce. Real sensitive language/cultural archives should not be loaded into an alpha protocol merely to demonstrate the graph.

## A beautiful observatory that people can actually read

The research does not establish that a graph canvas is the best home screen. Network visualization experiments vary by task and size: **Ren et al. (2019)** found node-link views better overall than two matrix variants for their 20/50-node social-network tasks (N=600), while **Abdelaal et al. (online 2022)** found matrices most reliable across their task set, including large 500-node overview tasks (N=150) [23,24]. Neither evaluates Papuan community knowledge, mobile accessibility or the visual design proposed here.

Therefore choose the presentation around what someone wants to learn, and evaluate it with actual readers. The following are **design hypotheses**, not findings of those studies.

| Direction | Experience | Strength / tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Reading landscape — recommended starting point** | A quiet place/language lens; one meaningful featured article or question; a few source-backed connections; “Baru ditambahkan” and “Perlu ditinjau” sections within the Wiki home. Follow a relationship as a readable trail. | Fits the current paper UI and Wikipedia-like reading. Useful before the graph is large. Requires editorial care; a featured selection must not imply comprehensive representation. |
| **Focal relationship view — optional** | Open an entity/article to a stable, small neighborhood. Label curves with readable relationship verbs; select one to inspect its evidence, perspective and date. Offer the same relations as a list. | Makes unexpected connections legible. Becomes cluttered quickly; expand on demand instead of showing the entire commons. An initial cap such as 8–12 visible neighbors is a prototype choice to test, not a scientifically proven threshold. |
| **Place/time observatory — later, where appropriate** | Approved map extents, optional time/season lens, observations and changes, with links back to articles and evidence. Show what was measured or reported, not simply a heat map of “knowledge.” | Connects lived landscapes and current events. Greater risk of sensitive location inference, false boundary precision and incomplete coverage. Needs approved data and meaningful community-defined indicators before it can be honest. |

### Specific composition and interaction ideas

- Keep the **paper surface, generous reading width and restrained ink**. Let an approved landscape print establish place at the top, fading into the page; do not use a decorative graph as a substitute for meaningful content. Do not manufacture an “Indigenous aesthetic” from sacred motifs or someone else's ontology.
- Lead with an ordinary invitation such as **“Jelajahi pengetahuan bersama”**, then the selected context and real pages. No dense manifesto, new pseudo-scientific terminology or mandatory ontology tutorial.
- A **trail** reads like `place → described in → recording excerpt → discussed by → community post`, with original relation wording available. Keep back navigation and a visible path; do not require pan/zoom to return to an article.
- Use cards for **actual questions, sources and relationships**, not only topic labels. A row might say “Apa yang berubah di sini?” and show a dated sourced observation beside its linked page. Do not invent a local question or implication merely to fill an empty state.
- Show **source and review status next to claims**. A provisional article can still be inviting to read. “Needs checking” should invite a specific correction or evidence contribution, rather than suggest that the entire community's knowledge awaits outside approval.
- A “different accounts” disclosure can place two **attributed descriptions side by side**, retaining the original wording and citations. Do not force a consensus or call the less popular account erroneous automatically.
- Colors can distinguish the selected trail or interaction state. Avoid giving each culture/ethnic group a fixed color, implying scientific certainty through saturation, or using only color to encode review/access state.
- On mobile, default to **readable stacked cards and a relationship list**. A focal graph can open as an optional view. Touch targets, legible edge labels, clear selection and a single evidence sheet matter more than physics effects.
- Provide keyboard-operable links/buttons, logical focus order, meaningful accessible names, textual descriptions of relationships, and the same evidence controls outside the canvas. Respect reduced motion and retain a stable layout; no continuous drift or spring animation while someone is reading.
- Do not measure “percentage of a culture documented,” rank knowledgeholders by graph centrality, or use article count as a completeness score. Coverage gaps may reflect consent, language access or missing work rather than missing knowledge.

The Wiki home can become visually rich **without shipping a graph runtime**: public article cards, a carefully sourced reading trail, approved prints, clear language/place lenses, source excerpts and recent changes already communicate a living landscape. A graph may later improve an actual task; it should earn its complexity.

## A staged next step after the current implementation run

**Stage 0 — discussion and custody decisions.** Read the owner's earlier notes. Identify one willing community/use case and who may approve which materials, terms and public relationships. Clarify which connections should remain narrative or not be recorded at all. This is not a blanket community approval requirement on ordinary public posting; it concerns new structured publication of community-governed knowledge.

**Stage 1 — paper prototype over already public material.** Select a handful of current sourced pages/notes, with independently approved relationships. Compare a plain reading list, a relational trail and a small focal graph. Use synthetic placeholders for protected material, clearly labeled, rather than importing it to make the demonstration interesting. Keep the existing accepted/provisional Wiki model and URLs.

**Stage 2 — prove utility and boundaries.** Can readers find a source, explain what a relationship means, notice a provisional claim, distinguish two perspectives, return to where they began and navigate on a phone/keyboard? Can an authorized steward correct one edge without erasing another perspective? Does public output remain identical when an inaccessible private relation is added? Test that source withdrawal removes derived suggestions and that public counts, layout, search and exports disclose nothing additional.

**Stage 3 — choose implementation only if those tasks improve.** Small assertion/vocabulary records with versioned public projections may be enough. Decide on RDF exports, Wikibase, selected standard mappings, or a graph store only for a demonstrated interchange/query need. Do not expand the competition demo into a promised universal ontology, live sensor network or automated cultural intelligence system.

Success is people finding and caring for meaningful relationships with evidence and control. It is not the number of edges, an impressive animated graph, or agreement with a model's clusters.

### Questions saved for the owner's later discussion

1. Which part of the owner's existing writing best captures the intended observatory, and which community questions should the first reading landscape answer?
2. Should the first useful lens be place, language, a present-day question, or a revisitable trail chosen by a community? Different entry points can coexist without one hierarchy owning the others.
3. Who can author, revise, attribute and approve a relationship in each real case? Where should differing accounts coexist, and where would representing a relation publicly itself be inappropriate?
4. What is the smallest useful public example from the current Wiki that demonstrates these connections without implying a community has endorsed a schema?
5. Which Node-held material, if any, has an approved public derivative suitable for linking? What remains entirely outside public discovery?
