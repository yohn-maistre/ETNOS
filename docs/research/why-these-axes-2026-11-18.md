# Why these axes

18 November 2026. Research for the owner. Tags: [V] verified on a page or file opened today, [S] from a search summary or memory, [U] unverified. Search summaries are marked [S] even when the source is good, because I read the summary and not the page. Nothing else in the repo was changed.

## Judge-ready answer

**English.** Strata does not invent its axes. Place, language, topic and kind answer the questions people have asked of any piece of knowledge for about two thousand years, which are where, who, what, what sort of thing and when. Rhetoric, library science, metadata standards and community studies each reached a similar short list on their own. We keep the axes fixed and let every community fill in its own values, such as its own places, its own languages and its own topic list. Rules about who may see something, and who holds it, sit in a separate protocol layer that only the community sets, because that is how Indigenous data projects such as Local Contexts and Mukurtu treat it. We have found two places where a short list falls short, seasonal time and named non-human beings, and we plan to close them as small extensions to the existing axes. (The owner has not decided this yet.)

**Bahasa Indonesia.** Sumbu Strata tidak kami karang sendiri. Tempat, bahasa, topik, dan jenis menjawab pertanyaan yang sudah lama diajukan orang tentang pengetahuan apa pun, yaitu di mana, siapa, apa, jenis apa, dan kapan. Ilmu retorika, ilmu perpustakaan, standar metadata, dan kajian komunitas masing-masing sampai pada daftar pendek yang mirip. Sumbunya tetap, dan nilainya diisi oleh setiap komunitas sendiri, misalnya tempat, bahasa, dan daftar topiknya. Aturan tentang siapa boleh melihat dan siapa pemegang pengetahuan ada di lapisan protokol yang terpisah dan hanya ditetapkan komunitas, sebagaimana dilakukan Local Contexts dan Mukurtu. Kami menemukan dua hal yang belum tertangkap daftar pendek itu, yaitu waktu musiman dan makhluk bukan manusia yang punya nama, dan keduanya kami rencanakan sebagai perluasan kecil pada sumbu yang sudah ada.

## Summary in ten lines

1. The hypothesis mostly holds. Five of the six lineages list dimensions that line up with Strata's axes. Two of the lead's details need correction (section 1).
2. Correction. The seven circumstances are now traced to Aristotle's Nicomachean Ethics. Hermagoras is a transmitter [V].
3. Correction. Wenger's own text says a neighbourhood is usually not a community of practice [V]. His model matches topic communities, not place communities.
4. Correction. Tönnies's three forms were not confirmed in the two pages I opened. Two summaries give kinship, place and shared belief or friendship [S].
5. Correction. Mukurtu has no Coverage field, and "communities" are not an item field [V].
6. "Axes universal, values local" holds. Language is not a child of place. BRWA, PARADISEC and Papuan Malay all show this (section 3).
7. Kinship and descent are covered by custody, and Local Contexts already has TK Clan and TK Family labels there [V]. Gender-restricted knowledge is also covered there.
8. Strongest real gap is cyclical time. Strata's time is a range. Maramataka, Aboriginal seasonal calendars and Dayak swidden cycles recur [S].
9. Second gap is named non-human beings (rivers, mountains, species) as entities with their own names and links. Partly covered by place and aliases.
10. Evidence is thin for Papua specifically. No Papuan knowledge archive found uses a dimension Strata lacks (section 5).

**Corrections to the lead's wording.**

- **Hermagoras.** Wikipedia says the circumstances were long attributed to Hermagoras, and that Aristotle's Nicomachean Ethics is now identified as the source. Pseudo-Augustine quotes Hermagoras using them as loci of an issue. "Quibus adminiculis" (by what means) and "quem ad modum" (in what way) are in his list. Cicero's De Inventione and Boethius carry them on [V]. The safe wording is "a tradition running from Aristotle through Hermagoras, Cicero and Boethius".
- **Tönnies.** The lead's three forms (blood, place, mind) match what two secondary sources say, with differing labels. One lists kinship, neighbourhood, town and spiritual community as prototypes [S]. I did not reach the 1887 text. Cite Tönnies as "kinship, shared place and shared belief" and flag the exact wording as unverified.
- **Wenger.** Fits topic communities. His text separates neighbourhoods from communities of practice [V]. Do not claim it describes place-based rooms.
- **Mukurtu.** The Dublin Core likeness is an inference. The docs page does not say which fields come from Dublin Core, and there is no Coverage field. Location fields do that work [V].

**What the convergence shows.** These lineages do not agree on one list. They agree that a record needs a few separate questions answered, and that who, where, what and when appear in every one. Language appears only in Dublin Core, Mukurtu and the archives, because it matters most where people hold several languages. Kinship appears in Tönnies, in Indonesian law and in Local Contexts, and in each case it sits next to authority, not next to topic. That supports Strata's choice to keep kinship out of labels and put it in custody. I did not find a source that says four axes is the right number. The claim to make is convergence, not proof.

## 2. Axes universal, values local

| Axis | Local values seen | Shared standard that works everywhere |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Place | Wilayah adat (BRWA registers 1,499 territories, 30.1 million ha, to 2024 [S]), terra indígena, iwi rohe, village and district | A place record with a name, aliases, a parent and optional boundary. The values come from each region. |
| Language | Mee, Biak, Papuan Malay, Indonesian | Glottolog gives each language, dialect and family a stable Glottocode in a family tree [V]. The page I read did not mention coordinates, so I cannot say it places languages on a map. |
| Topic | Each community's list. Ngā Upoko Tukutuku, Brian Deer, Mukurtu categories | A starter set labelled as a default, a way to rename and add, and crosswalks (see the 18 November topic note). |
| Kind | Cerita, kabar, tanya | A short fixed list in the engine. Communities rename it. |

**Language is not a child of place.** The evidence is consistent.

- PARADISEC records the language of the item, the language used in it, a language code, country and region or village as separate fields [S].
- BRWA lists "bahasa" as its own item next to community name, history, boundary and institutions. Its field is "bahasa yang digunakan secara turun temurun" [V, guide text]. The language belongs to the community, and the territory is another field.
- Papuan Malay is used across Papua as a lingua franca, and sources describe coastal and urban shift from local languages toward it [S]. A place page in Jayapura will carry posts in Malay, Indonesian and several heritage languages.
- Diaspora is already in the repo as a place entry (Perantauan).

Keep language as its own axis. A language may be shown under many places and a place under many languages. The only risk is a UI that nests one inside the other.

Local Contexts has TK Clan and TK Family as provenance labels for "the group or sub-group which is the primary cultural authority" [V]. BRWA's tenure kinds include "tanah keluarga/marga" [V]. Give the protocol or custody record a `holder` that a community may fill with a clan or family name, shown only as the community chooses (the Local Contexts provenance pattern). Do not build a kinship browser. The repo already holds plural authority in the custody design (see the November 1 language custody note). |
| More-than-human beings (species, rivers, mountains as kin or persons) | Descola's grid of interiority and physicality gives four ontologies (animism, totemism, analogism, naturalism). A Strata record ties place, people, topic and time to a teller and a source, and the claims table keeps competing accounts side by side. ## 5. Where the evidence is thin, and fair objections

**Thin evidence.**

- Papua. I found no study of how Papuans group knowledge, and no verified Papuan source for clan totems or garden and sago cycles. The Melanesian and Marind material is general [S].
- Most web evidence in this note is from search summaries. Items marked [S] should be opened before anyone quotes them. The NZ Acts and several library pages returned 403 or a bot check.
- Tönnies's three forms, Wilson's page claims and Descola's definitions are second-hand.
- The count of four axes has no source. The lineages show a recurring core, not a number.
- Whether communities will use a `season` or `holder` field is untested.

**Fair objections a judge could raise.**

1. "Your list came from European and North American theory." Fair for rhetoric, Ranganathan and Dublin Core. The answer is that the Indigenous systems checked (Local Contexts, Mukurtu, BRWA, Ngā Upoko Tukutuku, ISA) also separate place, language, subject and authority. They add custody and season, which Strata should also carry.
2. "Why not people?" Strata leaves peoples and clans out of labels to avoid profiling. Indonesian law and Tönnies both treat descent as basic. The reply is that descent is recorded as the holder of a protocol, chosen by the community, and never as a tag on a person.
3. "Your topics split nature from culture." Possible. The remedy is multi-label, community renaming and entity pages. The headings of the shipped list still need a check against this objection.
4. "Four is arbitrary." Yes. We claim convergence and a design reason (a place page filters by each).
5. "Seasonal time is missing." Agreed. It is the first extension.
6. "Rights-of-nature law proves what?" Only that some legal systems already treat rivers and mountains as persons. It does not say Papuan communities do. Ask them.

## 6. Recommendations

1. Use the judge answer above. Say "we found two gaps and are closing them" and name seasonal time.
2. Add an optional community-defined `season` to records. Cheapest real gap.
3. Add a `holder` to the protocol record, filled only by the community.
4. Allow entity pages (river, mountain, species) with multi-language names and typed links.
5. Check the topic headings for a nature and culture split before the showcase.
6. Ask Papuan stewards about totems, garden cycles and what they would not want labelled. This is the main open question for the owner.
