## Places become the spine

From `research/places-and-hierarchy-2026-11-16.md`. Proposal for the owner to confirm.

**Separate axes.** Place, language and topic each have their own tree and their own pages. Community kind ("Forum musyawarah") and institution kind become a field on a community or organisation and a filter chip, not a page. Page and post kinds (Cerita, Kabar) are fields too. Today the eleven community kinds and seven institution kinds are topic pages, which is why "Forum musyawarah" looks like a place.

**A real place tree.** Tanah Papua, then wilayah adat, then kabupaten or kota, then distrik, then kampung. Provinces are a second, administrative parent shown as a chip. Each place edge says what it is: `within` (the law: one parent) or `reference` (a planning region: up to three, with a source line and whole or part). A regency in two regions (Nabire) is listed on both, hatched on the map, and counted once. Distrik and kampung are loaded for search but appear in browse only when something uses them. "Wilayah acuan, bukan batas tanah adat" stays on every region.

**Navigation.** Beranda, Tempat, Komunitas, Wiki, Jelajah. Tempat answers where and what is here; Komunitas who talks; Wiki what is known; they meet on the place page. Topic and language directories live in Jelajah.

**One map with a scope.** The wiki home map, the place page "inti lapisan", the news map and the explore map become one component with a scope (whole site or one place) and a lens. Five components today (PlaceMap, PlaceStrata, CoreSample, KnowledgeMap, NewsMap) to be merged where they can.

**First-time user.** Four skippable cards after first sign-in: Dekat mana (up to three places, never inferred, "bukan bukti tempat tinggal"), Bahasa, Minat, then a starter bundle (three rooms, two pages, the region's Aksara). Nothing is joined until the person taps. Beranda gets "Untuk kamu" and "Semua".

**First deployment.** Aksara drafts, a person confirms each step, everything carries a source:

1. Frame: the operator names the root place, bounds and interface languages.
2. Places from Wikidata, checked against OSM and the Kemendagri and BPS codes; disagreements shown side by side.
3. Reference regions from the planning documents the steward names, quoted.
4. Languages from Glottolog.
5. Topics from the template, renamed locally, two levels.
6. One open room per region; community kinds with their admission defaults.
7. Wiki stubs per region, regency and language, live and marked unreviewed.
8. Agent switches per region at a low daily limit; handover of rooms to local moderators.

The site stays out of search and agents stay quiet until steps 1 to 4 are confirmed. Studio shows this as a "Mulai dari awal" rail with conflicts, a diff before each load, and receipts.

## A pipeline others can run

The seed was built with scripts in this repository. To make it a pipeline any deployment can run:

1. `seed.config.json` per deployment: root place, regions and their pages, languages, source tiers, blocked hosts.
2. Survey step (agent with web access) writes `seed/wiki/<region>.json`.
3. `scripts/verify-wiki-seed.py` keeps only sentences found word for word.
4. Writer step (Gemma 4 or Flash-Lite through the desk pipeline) writes pages from sentences; `scripts/check-wiki-pages.ts` enforces names, numbers and citations; failing pages go back once.
5. `scripts/propose-wiki-pages.ts` publishes through the agent switch.
6. `scripts/observatory/build-map.mjs` rebuilds Peta wiki; later a scheduled job.

Each step writes a file a person can read and commit, so a community can inspect and correct before anything goes live.

## Biodiversity, artifacts and detak-detik

From `research/detak-detik-and-biocultural-sources-2026-11-16.md`.

**Taken from Detak Detik** (the owner's automated newspaper): a reviewed registry as the floor that live fetches only add to; licence, creator, source and date stored with every image; a source ledger where a source that goes dark becomes a visible gap; a fact gate written as code, here extended to every number and year (Detak Detik's gate skipped numbers under 100); absence shown as content; the dotted map and engraved plate styles. Not copied: sample numbers shown as real, Wikipedia as the only text for a people.

**Data checked on 16 November:** GBIF holds 608,324 records in a Western New Guinea box (210,352 of them preserved specimens; CC0, CC BY and CC BY-NC by dataset). IUCN data may not be redistributed, so only the category label is shown and linked. Wikidata has Indonesian species names but almost no Papuan-language names. Wereldmuseum publishes CC0 linked data with acquisition events; the Met's Asmat records are open metadata but its sampled images are not public domain; the British Museum and quai Branly have no usable open API. The national intangible heritage registry (WBTb) lists 2,727 items, and the Papua provinces submitted none in 2025. Copernicus DEM and GeoNames are usable with credit.

**Features, in the suggested order:**

1. **Source ledger and a sensitivity field** on every record (`open`, `hidden-image`, `existence-only`) with a change log.
2. **Jejak Spesimen**: where Papua's natural-history specimens are held, who collected them and when, on a world map with the two-clock band. Clean open data; builds the holdings map that the next features reuse.
3. **Lembar Medan**: engraved terrain plates as the hero of a place page, local names first, printable at A3 and used as the share card.
4. **Foto Lama, Nama Baru**: 1950s colonial photographs beside the original caption and what people say today, as Beda versi pairs. **Daftar yang Tidak Ada**: the heritage registry and its silences, shown as a table.
5. **Peta Benda**: where Papuan objects are held worldwide, with origin, collector, date made and date acquired. Starts with Wereldmuseum, then the Met.
6. **Nama Hidup**: species pages that open with names in Indonesian and Papuan languages and the stories and uses attached, starting with about 50 species in three languages through partners. **Kalender Hidup**: a seasonal calendar for one place where signs told by people sit beside observation months, community entries first.
7. **Pulang**: a repatriation tracker, as editorial work.
8. **Satu Lembah**: one valley told through all of the above, done by hand for the December showcase.

**Care, aligned with the owner's surface-first decision:** objects and practices are surfaced with a summary and their source. Human remains (skulls, bones, mummies, hair) are recorded as existing, with holder and date acquired, never with images and never with a precise find spot. Images of objects a source marks sacred are shown only where the holding institution publishes them openly, and a community can flag them for hiding. Sacred sites can be marked "do not show on the plate"; their coordinates round to regency level when asked.

**Open questions for the owner:** where to draw the non-commercial line (IUCN, CC BY-NC data and some museum terms depend on it); who in Papua is the first partner on what is sacred; whether to write to IUCN, the British Museum, quai Branly and the Ministry of Culture (one page each).
